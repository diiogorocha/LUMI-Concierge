## E-MAIL 2 · The rides that run out

**Envio:** dia 4
**Assunto A:** The ride your kids want most can run out
**Assunto B:** What an auto-booking app can't do
**Preview:** Some Lightning Lanes are gone early. Here's why it matters.

Hi {{first_name}},

Last time I promised to tell you about the rides that run out. Here it is.

**Some of the most-wanted rides run out of Lightning Lanes early in the day.**

If you didn't grab one in time, you have two choices: a long standby line, or telling your kids "not today."

Now put that together with what we talked about last time:

- You can only book 3 before the day.
- After that, your next booking only opens once you scan in at a ride.
- The later it gets, the later the available times, and the fuller the park.
- Rides far apart make it worse. Every walk across the park pushes the next time further away.

And if you're traveling with grandparents, cousins or another family, it's harder still. A family of 3 needs 3 spots at the same time. A family of 8 needs 8.

That's how a day turns into a mix of Lightning Lanes and standby lines, long gaps between rides, and the one ride everyone wanted, missed.

**So what actually fixes it?**

Not a better spreadsheet.

What works is someone who knows which rides run out first and goes after those, books the next ride the second it's possible, keeps checking for earlier times, and keeps the next ride close to where you already are. Then does it again after the next ride. And the next. For 6 or 7 hours.

You may have seen auto-booking apps that do part of this. They can be useful. But an app books. It doesn't decide.

It doesn't know that your 4-year-old just got scared on a dark ride and needs something gentle next. It doesn't know a ride closed at 11 and your whole morning needs a new order. It doesn't know grandma needs to sit down.

That part takes a person.

**Want to see how a live park day works, step by step?** Just reply to this email with the word **HOW** and I'll send it to you.
