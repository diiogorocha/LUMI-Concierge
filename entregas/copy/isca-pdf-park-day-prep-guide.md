## PAGE 2 · IF YOU'RE THE DESIGNATED PLANNER…

# If you're the designated planner, this is for you.

Picture it.

You got the whole family to the gate before opening. Rope drop: done. You rode two rides with short standby lines. The kids are thrilled.

And now you're standing in the middle of the park, staring at your phone. Your first Lightning Lane isn't for a while. You can't book the next one until you use it. And the ride your kids care about most is already showing later and later times.

If that's you, you're not alone. In a July 2026 survey of 2,000 American women, 80% said they're the ones who do most of the trip planning.¹

So let's make your job easier.

**What this guide WILL do:**

- Help your family agree on what matters most, before you're standing in a park arguing about it.
- Get you ready for booking day (yes, the 7 AM one).
- Give you a simple checklist for park morning.
- Show you why the day after your first 3 Lightning Lanes is where things drift, and what it takes to keep it on track.

**What this guide WON'T do:**

It won't give you ride times or a "perfect" ride order.

Nobody honest can. Availability changes all day. It depends on crowds, on how big your group is, on where you are in the park, and on which rides are running that day. Anyone who hands you a fixed schedule weeks in advance is guessing.

You'll see exactly why on page 7.

## PAGE 3 · HOW LIGHTNING LANE WORKS RIGHT NOW (IN 60 SECONDS)

# How Lightning Lane works right now (in 60 seconds)

Lightning Lane is the paid system that lets you use a shorter line at a set return time. In plain English, there are three ways to use it:

| Option | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Lightning Lane Multi Pass** | Book **up to 3 rides before your park day**. After that, you book **one more at a time**, inside the park, once you've scanned in at a ride you booked |
| **Lightning Lane Single Pass** | One visit to one of the most in-demand rides, bought separately |
| **Lightning Lane Premier Pass** | Use the Lightning Lane at many rides without booking times; limited availability |

**When can you book your first 3?**

| If you're… | Your booking window opens… | Covers… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staying at a Disney resort hotel | **7 days** before check-in, at **7:00 AM Eastern** | Your whole stay |
| Staying anywhere else | **3 days** before each park day, at **7:00 AM Eastern** | The length of your ticket |

**What 7:00 AM Eastern means where you live:**

| Your time zone | Your alarm |
| --- | --- |
| Eastern (New York, Orlando) | 7:00 AM |
| Central (Chicago, Dallas) | 6:00 AM |
| Mountain (Denver) | 5:00 AM |
| Arizona (most of the state) | 4:00 AM from March to early November; 5:00 AM the rest of the year |
| Pacific (Los Angeles, Seattle) | **4:00 AM** |
| Alaska | 3:00 AM |
| Hawaii | 1:00 AM from March to early November; 2:00 AM the rest of the year |
| United Kingdom | 12:00 PM (noon), except for a week or two in late March and late October/early November, when it's **11:00 AM** because the U.S. and the U.K. change their clocks on different dates |

> **The key thing to remember:** your first 3 are booked from home. **Everything after that is booked inside the park, one at a time.** That's where most of your day is decided.
>
> **Rules change.** Always double-check options, windows and booking rules in the official My Disney Experience app before booking day. This page was checked on October 14, 2026.

## PAGE 4 · STEP 1: THE FAMILY MUST-DO LIST

# Step 1: Make your Family Must-Do List

This is the most useful 20 minutes of your whole trip prep. Do it at the kitchen table, with everyone.

**How it works:**

1. Each person picks their **top 3** for each park day.
2. Sort every pick into one of three levels:
   - **Would cry if we missed it**
   - **Really want**
   - **Bonus, if time allows**
3. Check height requirements and "is this too scary for the little ones?" now, not in line.
4. Circle your family's **Top 5**. If the day only goes halfway right, these are the ones that still have to happen.

## PAGE 5 · STEP 2: BOOKING WINDOW CHECKLIST

# Step 2: Get ready for booking day

Booking morning is a race against everyone else who opened the app at the same minute. Most of the stress comes from things you can set up ahead of time.

**The week before your window opens**

- [ ] The official My Disney Experience app is installed and you're logged in.
- [ ] Park tickets for **everyone** in your group are linked, so the whole group shows up when you book.
- [ ] If you're staying at a Disney resort hotel, your reservation is linked too (that's what gives you the 7-day window).
- [ ] You've decided **who** in the family is booking, so two people don't fight over the same rides.
- [ ] Your Family Must-Do List (page 4) is printed or open on a second screen.
- [ ] You know your window date: ____ / ____ and your local time for 7:00 AM ET: ________.

**The night before**

- [ ] Phone fully charged and on Wi-Fi.
- [ ] Alarm set **10 minutes before** your 7:00 AM ET time.
- [ ] Coffee ready. Seriously.

**On booking morning: choosing your first 3**

- [ ] Open the app a few minutes early and stay logged in.
- [ ] Put the "would cry if we missed it" rides first. Bonus rides can wait.
- [ ] Look at **times**, not just rides. Your next booking only opens after you scan in, so a first ride close to when you walk in helps the rest of the day move.
- [ ] Look at **where** the rides are. Three rides at opposite ends of the park means a lot of walking between them.
- [ ] Screenshot your confirmations.

## PAGE 6 · STEP 3: PARK MORNING CHECKLIST

# Step 3: Get ready for park morning

The best park days are won before you walk through the gate.

**Phones and power**

- [ ] Phone charged to 100%, plus a portable battery and cable.
- [ ] Decide **who holds the phone** today. (Hint: it doesn't have to be you.)
- [ ] App notifications turned on.

**Kids and energy**

- [ ] Snacks and refillable water bottles packed.
- [ ] A plan for the nap or the "quiet hour" (stroller nap, hotel break, or a slow show).
- [ ] Little ones too short or too scared for a ride? Ask a Cast Member how **Rider Switch** works, so the grown-ups can take turns without waiting in line twice.
- [ ] Sunscreen, hats, ponchos. Florida weather changes fast.

**The group**

- [ ] A meeting spot if anyone gets separated, and every kid knows it.
- [ ] Everyone has a photo of the Top 5 list.
- [ ] Agree on one rule for the day: when plans change, we go back to the Top 5.

**Your arrival time**

- [ ] Check your park's opening time for your date. Parks often open somewhere between 8:00 and 9:30 AM.
- [ ] Write down what time you plan to be at the gate: ________
- [ ] Write down your first Lightning Lane time: ________

## PAGE 7 · WHY THE DAY DRIFTS AFTER YOUR FIRST 3 (THE MECHANISM)

# Your first 3 Lightning Lanes are the easy part

You did everything right. You made the list. You woke up for 7 AM. You booked your 3. So why does the day still drift?

Because the real game starts after those 3. Here's how it works.

**1. The rope drop trap.**
Lots of families get to the park at opening to ride a few short standby lines. Smart move. But then the short lines are gone, and the family is standing around waiting for the time on their first Lightning Lane.

**2. You can only book the next one after you scan in.**
Before the day, you get 3. After that, your next booking only opens once you've scanned in at a ride you booked. So if your first ride is late, everything after it starts late too.

**3. The later it gets, the later the times.**
As the day goes on, available times keep moving later and the park keeps filling up. Rides far apart make it worse: every walk across the park pushes the next available time further away. And the bigger your group, the harder it is to find a time that works for everyone.

**4. Some must-do rides run out early.**
The Lightning Lanes for the most-wanted rides can be gone early in the day. If you didn't get one in time, your choices are a long standby line or skipping it.

## PAGE 8 · THE RE-BOOKING GAME

# The re-booking game

**Dead time** is the gap between leaving one ride and the next return time you're holding. It's the hour you spend wandering, the walk across the park "just to be close," the kids asking "what now?"

Cutting dead time is what makes a park day feel easy. Here's what it actually takes:

- **Knowing which rides run out early**, and booking those before they're gone.
- **Booking the next ride the moment it's possible**, right after you scan in. Not 20 minutes later, when you finally have a free hand.
- **Checking availability all day long**, not just at 7 AM. Earlier times can open up at any moment, and you move to them without losing the ride.
- **Following your family's priority order**, so the "would cry if we missed it" rides are protected first.
- **Avoiding zig-zags.** Keeping the next ride close to where you are, so you're not crossing the park for a time slot while the times climb.
- **Adjusting on the fly** when a ride closes, it starts to rain, or someone needs a nap.
- **Doing it again after every single ride**, for 6 or 7 hours.

It's not hard because you're doing it wrong. It's hard because it's a full-time job running in the background of your vacation.

# Your honest options

There's no single right answer. Here's how the choices really compare.

| | **Do it yourself** | **Auto-booking app** | **Premier Pass** | **Live human strategist** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What it does** | You book, check and adjust all day | Searches and books return times for you in the background | Lets you use the Lightning Lane at many rides without booking times | A real person books your next ride the moment it's possible, moves bookings earlier and adjusts the day with you, in your group chat |
| **What it doesn't do** | Give you your phone back | Decide what matters most to your family, know which rides run out early on a given day, or react to a closed ride, a nap or a meltdown | Help with rides it doesn't cover, or plan the day for you; it's limited | Guarantee specific times or rides; nobody can |
| **Best for** | Planners who enjoy the game | Experienced families who mainly want bookings made | Small groups with a big budget and a short visit | Families who want someone running the day while they're present |

**If you're doing it yourself, three tips that help:**

1. **Book the next ride right after you scan in.** That's the first moment you can, and every minute counts.
2. **Do one area of the park at a time.** Try to book the next ride close to where you are now.
3. **Protect the Top 5.** When something goes wrong, go back to the list and let the bonus rides go.

You've got this. And if you'd rather not spend the day doing it, there's another way.
